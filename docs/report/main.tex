This chapter details the practical realization of the HydraSAT framework, documenting the transition from a conceptual design to a functional distributed system. 
We describe the project’s evolution through several key development phases, highlighting the technical milestones achieved and the engineering decisions prompted by infrastructure constraints.
\section{Preliminary work}
\vspace{-0.5cm}
Before embarking on the implementation of the framework, we conducted a comprehensive preliminary study to establish a rigorous theoretical foundation. 
Our investigation focused on the mechanics of modern SAT and {\#}SAT solvers. 
Specifically, we analyzed the Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) algorithm and compared chronological versus non-chronological backtracking strategies for solving propositional satisfiability problems \cite{chrono-sat}. 
Furthermore, we surveyed the current landscape of SAT methodology, including exact and approximate model counting techniques, component decomposition, and hashing-based approaches \cite{count-sat-handbook}. 
This theoretical grounding was essential for designing a distributed architecture capable of handling complex model-counting tasks.
